## 1. Purpose, in plain words

Four things, in the order they protect the customer:

1. **Legal deadlines survive an outage (G18, G19).** Every organisation has an offline copy of its open reporting deadlines: a daily e-mail and a download. After an outage, people back-enter what arrived, with a late-entry reason and a duplicate check. A clock check proves the timestamps on audit entries and signatures are right.
2. **Pages are fast enough for a pilot (speed, G7, boundary tests).** The complaint page and the story page meet the approved targets. Every release runs the browser journeys and the load checks, and every security limit is tested on both sides.
3. **The customer can review and sign what auditors ask for (G1, G14, G21, G10).** An audit trail review with red flags, a periodic access review, a configuration report with a comparison, and a reason on every critical edit after a signed decision.
4. **Show where a value came from, and what MDARIX saves (G20, P1, P2, navigation).** "Trace this value", possible duplicates by similar wording, outcome numbers with a baseline, and a navigation review.

**Rules that hold everywhere:** signed records are never rewritten; MDARIX never decides, signs or submits for a person; no AI anywhere in B6a; no patient or reporter data in e-mails or queue payloads; every new route is tenant-scoped under forced RLS; no competitor names, no prices.

## 3. Part A: deadline protection (G18, G19) — first

### 3.1 The daily deadline list (G18a)
- **What it lists:** every open reporting item of the organisation, from the same reads as Oversight: market clocks being assessed, reports owed and not yet submitted, provisional clocks from the watch (categories `REPORTING` and `OBLIGATION`, `oversight/rules.py:46-54`), overdue first, then soonest. Per line: complaint reference, market, what is owed (decision or report type), due date and time with its zone (`due_label`), state (overdue / due soon / later), owner's name.
- **Never in it:** patient, reporter or description text (as the Oversight CSV, `oversight/router.py:665`).
- **The e-mail:** a scheduler job `deadline-list`, every day including weekends (clocks run on weekends), from 06:00 in the organisation's time zone (oversight calendar). One outbox event `DEADLINE_LIST` per organisation per day; the queue holds references and dates only, names and addresses are resolved at send (`notifications.py:3-5`). Idempotent by `(tenant, day)` like the readiness digest. An empty list is still sent ("0 open reporting deadlines today"), so silence means a problem, not "nothing due" (⚑ B6a-D1).
- **Who gets it (⚑ B6a-D1):** everyone with regulatory approval, Leaders and customer administrators. A person may switch it off under My account (new key `daily_deadlines` in the existing `email_preferences` JSON, no migration), unless they are the last recipient left on.
- **The download:** `GET /api/v1/oversight/deadlines.csv` and `.pdf` (`data.export`, as the CSV export), audited `DEADLINE_LIST_DOWNLOADED`. The PDF is one printable page per 40 lines, dated, with "Printed copy for an outage: check MDARIX when it is back".

### 3.2 Back-entry after an outage (G18b, G18c)
- `IntakeIn` gains `late_entry_reason`. When the awareness time (or, if none, the received time) is more than **24 hours** before entry (⚑ B6a-D2), the reason is required: 422 `LATE_ENTRY_REASON_NEEDED`, at least `REASON_MIN` (10) characters (`intake/rules.py`). Stored in the existing column; audited; shown on the Case as "Entered late: <reason>".
- **The clock counts from awareness, never from entry** (today's rule, `intake/rules.py:13-15`). A test proves a back-entered Case shows its real, possibly overdue, deadline.
- **Duplicate check before save:** `GET /api/v1/intake/possible-duplicates` with product, lot, serial, event date and site/country runs today's rule (`intake/rules.py:721`) against Cases, complaints and e-mails received meanwhile (B7b). The form shows matches before "Save"; the person links (existing route) or saves anyway. No automatic merge.

### 3.3 Manual-workaround SOP template (G18d)
A short starter template, downloadable from Help: when to switch to manual mode; use the last e-mailed list or printout; a paper/spreadsheet log whose columns match the Case form; who decides reportability meanwhile; how to back-enter with the reason; who checks completeness after recovery. Marked "starter: adapt to your own procedures; to confirm with your consultant" (⚑ B6a-D15). It does not tell anyone how to report to an authority: it points to the customer's own SOP.

### 3.4 Clock health check (G19)
- **In the app:** readiness gains a `clock` check: app time vs database `clock_timestamp()` (corrected for the round trip), and, when `MDARIX_NTP_SERVER` is set, the NTP offset by one standard-library SNTP query (no new dependency; "unknown" when the network blocks it). Above **1 s** (⚑ B6a-D7) the check is degraded; `operations_alerts` alerts once and once when it clears (pattern `scheduler.py:209-229`). The public status page is not changed.
- **For IQ and the restore drill:** `scripts/clock_check.py` prints and saves (artifacts JSON) the app, database and NTP offsets and, on the host, the output of `chronyc tracking`. Added to the release IQ steps, the deployment runbook and the restore drill (`docs/runbooks/BACKUP_AND_RESTORE.md`).
- **A deviation** above the limit: the alert text says "check the audit entries and signatures made since <last good check>"; the investigation itself is the customer's / MDARIX's procedure (B6b, G16). Records are never re-timed.

## 4. Part B: speed and release tests (S1-S4, G7, boundary tests)

### 4.1 The real causes (from the code, section 2)
1. **One page computes the whole organisation.** The complaint page asks for one complaint's dated items but `_item_views` and `_watch_clocks` read every complaint's (`oversight/router.py:393-394`).
2. **The watch runs twice per page** (`oversight/service.py:347` inside `due_items`, and `oversight/router.py:462`).
3. **The watch reads up to 20,000 complaints in Python** (`reportability/service.py:26, 354`) and the triage table for "needs" rows (`:412`).
4. **The story loads every imported complaint in full** (`trial/one_story.py:63-68`) and repeats cause 1 for all top-harm complaints (`:96-100`).
Payload size is not the problem (one complaint's view); query count and repeated whole-organisation work are.

### 4.2 The fix (smallest that works; no cache, no migration first)
- `reportability.watch(..., complaint_ids=None)`: when given, `_rows` filters by id in SQL. `due_items(..., complaint_ids=None)`: each source filters by complaint id; tasks and items with no complaint are skipped for a complaint read. `complaint_work` passes its ids and **reads the watch once**, shared by `due_items` and `_watch_clocks`.
- **Equivalence test** (the guard against wrong deadlines): for every complaint of the sample and of the 5,000-row load set, the scoped result equals the whole-organisation result filtered to that complaint (same items, same order, same dates).
- **Story:** pick the top-harm imported complaints in SQL (harm rank as a SQL expression, only id and dates), compute clocks for at most the 50 soonest-aware of them with the scoped read, and compute `most_urgent` once per request for both runs. Test: the same complaint is chosen as before on the load set.
- **Only if still above target after re-measuring:** a short per-request memo, then (owner decision) a precomputed watch. Not planned now.

## 5. Part C: reviews the customer signs (G1, G14, G21, G10)

### 5.1 One way to sign a review (no migration)
A review's content (period, filters, findings, the list reviewed) is built as JSON, hashed (SHA-256) and written as one audit event (`AUDIT_TRAIL_REVIEW_SIGNED`, `ACCESS_REVIEW_SIGNED`, `CONFIGURATION_SNAPSHOT_SIGNED`) holding the content in `details`. The signature is a `SignedApprovalRecord` (`db/models/foundation.py:1040-1070`) for that event, with `content_fingerprint` = the hash, after `verify_signing_credentials` (password + MFA, `governance_router.py:439`) and signer training (`signer_training/`). The audit chain makes the stored content tamper-evident. A PDF is rendered from the stored content. Nothing is ever edited after signing; a correction is a new review that names the earlier one (B6a-D6).

### 5.2 G1 audit trail review
- **Screen** (customer admin area, beside today's Audit section, `frontend/src/features/customer-admin/sections/Audit.tsx`): choose the period (default: the month since the last signed review). **Flags first**, each with a count and a list:
  1. edits without a reason (edit actions whose `reason` is empty);
  2. edits after a signature (an event on a record that already had a signed record);
  3. data changes by administrator accounts or by MDARIX staff (staff shown as "MDARIX platform administrator", `observability.py:58`);
  4. bulk changes (one person, more than 50 changes in 10 minutes) and changes outside the working calendar;
  5. failed sign-in spikes (10 or more `SIGN_IN_FAILED` for one account in a day, `auth_router.py:825`);
  6. the chain check result (`verify_tenant_chain`).
- Then all events of the period, paged, with old/new values. The reviewer writes findings (or "none") and signs.
- Reads the control **and** data-plane events (risk in section 2).
- **Who signs (⚑ B6a-D4):** Leader or Quality Reviewer. A reviewer whose own actions are among the flags cannot sign those flags off alone: a second reviewer confirms them.
- A review falls due on the schedule (monthly by default) as an Oversight item ("Audit trail review due").

### 5.3 G14 periodic access review
- One row per active user: base role, extra and custom roles, MFA on, last active, invited by and when, role changes in the period. The reviewer marks **Keep**, **Change** or **Remove** per row. Change and Remove use today's routes (audited as now); the review records what was decided.
- Every row must be marked before signing. A reviewer's own row is marked by another administrator, or by a Leader when there is only one administrator (⚑ B6a-D5).
- Due every 90 days by default (Oversight item). Signed by the customer administrator.

### 5.4 G21 configuration report and comparison
- **Snapshot** of the configuration in force: reporting windows and look-back, role per market, personal-data setting, rule-register versions and floors in force, switches (`feature_set.py`), oversight calendar and escalation, complaint review settings, communication settings, e-mail intake address and settings, AI setting, signal rules, connectors and mapping versions (never keys), users and roles (from 5.3). Download as PDF and JSON; "Sign this snapshot" uses 5.1.
- **Comparison** of two snapshots (two signed ones, or a signed one and "now"): each changed item with before and after, and who, when and why from the audit events between the two dates (the settings routes already audit old/new values and most require a reason).
- `configuration_baselines` is left untouched (platform template, section 2).

### 5.5 G10 reason for change on critical edits
Inventory of every path that changes a fact a signed decision used:
| Path | Today | B6a |
|---|---|---|
| Reporting facts: country, awareness date (`assessment_router.py:781-858`) | No reason | Reason required (⚑ B6a-D3: always), in the audit `reason` |
| Re-upload of a complaints file (`trial/imports.py:2297-2305`) | Field history, no reason | The check step lists complaints whose signed conclusion used a changed field (country, awareness, event date, death, serious injury, product, lot); one reason for the run, stored with the run's audit and field changes |
| QMS connector sync | Source-of-record update | Reason recorded automatically as "QMS sync <run>"; shown in G1 flag 2 |
| Case fact correction (`intake/router.py:3297`) | Reason required | No change |
| Re-signing a market conclusion (`assessment_router.py:580-588`) | Change reason required | No change |

**After any such change**, every signed conclusion of that complaint whose inputs differ now reads "Facts changed after signing: re-check" (computed on read from the conclusion's stored `date_calc` and market against the complaint; nothing signed is rewritten). Re-checking is the existing re-sign with a change reason.

## 6. Part D: trace, similar wording, outcome numbers, navigation (G20, P1, P2)

### 6.1 G20 "Trace this value"
On a report page and its frozen file view, each value has a small "Trace" link. The panel shows, in order: the worksheet row's source (`worksheet.py:1129`) → the complaint field; that field's history (field changes, `db/models/intake.py:437-454`; audit old/new values, who and when); the import that brought it (file, line, mapping version: `ingestion.py:175-189`); the frozen snapshot it is in (who approved, when, SHA-256). Read only. `GET /api/v1/reports/{id}/trace/{field}`. If the audit read by entity is above 500 ms at 100,000 audit events, an index-only migration is added (B6a-D12).

### 6.2 P1 possible duplicates by similar wording
- For a Case or complaint, candidates are bounded in the database: same organisation, same product (or no product), event within 90 days, at most 500, not already linked or dismissed.
- Score: shared words / all words of the two descriptions, after today's `normalize` (`complaint_review/rules.py`) and a short stop-word list. At **0.5** or above (B6a-D8) the pair is suggested with the reason "similar description (62 % words in common)", beside today's rule reasons, in the duplicates list and the escalation panel.
- A person confirms or dismisses with today's audited link and undo. No AI; nothing merged automatically.
- `shortcut:` word overlap misses typos; upgrade to `pg_trgm` if precision on real pilot data is low (needs an extension and a migration).

### 6.3 P2 outcome numbers with baselines (Value Centre v0)
- Monthly, from timestamps already recorded (never people's time): received → triage complete; awareness → every market concluded (signed); conclusion → report approved and submitted; reports on time (% of due dates met, `report_rules.on_time`); complaint open → closed (event closure); CAPA effectiveness checks done on time. Median and 90th percentile, with counts.
